**The Ashborne's Charge** 

I remember what was done. I rise from what remains. I purify what corrupts. I am not what was made of me. I am what refused to become it. 


### **WHO MORTHEON CHOOSES** 

Mortheon does not maintain a large clergy. He is not that kind of god. He chooses from among those who have been unmade and rebuilt — the transformed, the violated, the survivors of forces that attempted to make them into something they did not consent to be. Specifically, he chooses the ones who did not let the cold win. Who held their own names in their own minds through everything that was done to take those names away. Who came out of whatever underground chamber the world put them in and stood up afterward and chose, deliberately, to remain themselves — and then chose, further, to spend that remaining self on the work of making sure it happened to fewer people. He rarely chooses. Perhaps a dozen Ashborne exist in the world at any given time, spread across civilizations, working without hierarchy or formal structure, finding each other occasionally by the recognition of the brand and the fire behind the eyes and the particular quality of stillness that belongs to people who have been through the worst and come out of it with their warmth intact. He demands everything of the people he chooses. He does not soften this or apologize for it. The work is not small and the fire he gives is not decorative — it is functional, surgical, designed for the specific purpose of finding corruption and removing it. The Ashborne are not soldiers in the conventional sense. They are more like cauterization — precise, painful, final, and ultimately in the service of healing.**

## ORIGIN 

 The First Burning Before he was Mortheon, he was nothing with a name. He was a fire spirit — minor, local, the kind of small divine presence that inhabits a particular hearth or a hillside flame or the warmth at the center of a forge. Ancient even then, as such things measure age, but without significance. Without purpose beyond the nature of fire itself: to burn, to warm, to consume what it is given. What he was given, in an age so old that no civilization alive possesses even the ruins of the civilization that preceded it, was suffering on a scale he had no framework to contain. Deity Reference Document 1 MORTHEON — The Ashborne | Deity Reference The details are lost. This is not an accident — what was done was done thoroughly, by something that understood that memory is the enemy of atrocity and acted accordingly. What survives is the shape of it: a people, numerous and without apparent defense against what came for them, taken and transformed against their will across generations. The fire spirit felt it the way fire feels fuel — as an intake, vast and sudden, too large for what he was. The agony of a people poured into something too small to hold it. Mortheon ascended. He did not emerge from the suffering as its representative or its monument. He emerged as its consequence — the thing that happens when enough wrongness accumulates in one place that the universe itself develops an immune response. He is not the god of those people. They are gone, name and language and culture and memory, more completely than anything else in recorded or unrecorded history. He is what their suffering became when it had nowhere else to go. He remembers what was done. He is the only thing that does. The memory lives in him the way heat lives in stone — absorbed, structural, part of what he is made of now, released slowly and at great temperature when something presses against it. He has been burning ever since. Patiently. Waiting for the people who would understand what he is and choose, with full knowledge of the cost, to carry the fire forward.

The eye itself is depicted without iris or pupil — a blank, burning witness, seeing everything, recording everything, forgetting nothing. The flames around it reach upward and outward in seven points, uneven, as natural fire is uneven, as though the eye is not decorated with flame but is genuinely on fire and has been for a very long time and intends to remain so. It is simple enough to brand into skin. Simple enough to strike into coin, carve into stone, press into wax. It reads immediately as what it is: something that has seen the worst the world can do and has not looked away and will not look away and is not finished yet

# APPEARANCE

Mortheon is terrifying to behold. This is not incidental. It is the truth of what he is. He manifests as an enormous man — not gigantic, not monstrous in proportion, but wrong in scale in a way that takes a moment to identify, as though the space he occupies is slightly larger than the body that occupies it. He is broad through the shoulder and chest in the way of something built for force rather than display. His skin is the color of cooling embers — deep red brown shot through with lines of brighter orange that pulse with his breathing, as though the fire that made him is still present in his veins and has not fully decided whether to stay contained. In darkness he glows. Not warmly. With the particular deep red of a forge at full heat — the color that precedes white, the color that means the temperature has moved past the point where things remain solid. His eyes are the worst of it. They are entirely fire — not eyes with fire in them, not pupils lit from within, but fire occupying the space where eyes should be. Deep red at the edges shifting to white-gold at the absolute center, unwavering, unblinking, seeing with the completeness of something that has been watching since before memory began. To meet his gaze directly is to feel seen in a way that has nothing to do with being looked at and everything to do with being known — every act, every choice, every moment of surrender to the cold and every moment of resistance to it, present and accounted for in that witness. He does not smile. He is not warm in the way that warmth is comfortable. He is warm in the way that a forge is warm — true, total, and not designed with your comfort as its primary consideration. When he speaks, his voice arrives before the sound does — felt in the chest as pressure before it is heard as words, the way thunder arrives in the body before the ears process it. 

  

Presence When Mortheon manifests, fires nearby shift to deep red, then orange, then back, as though acknowledging him. The air carries the smell of heated iron and something beneath it — older, darker, the specific smell of stone that has been subjected to extreme heat and survived

# COMMANDMENTS/TENETS

## The Three Fires of Mortheon 

#### I. Remember What Was Done 

You are not required to forgive. You are not required to make peace with the violation of yourself or others. You are required to remember it clearly, completely, and without the softening that time and comfort offer. Memory is the first fire. It does not negotiate. It does not go out. 


#### II. Rise from What Remains 

What was taken cannot always be returned. What was broken cannot always be mended whole. Ash is not nothing — ash is what fire leaves behind when it has finished its work, and from ash, things grow. You are not what you were before. You are what remained when everything that could be burned away was burned away. That is not diminishment. That is refinement. That is, in the language of the forge, the point. 


#### III. Purify What Corrupts 

There are forces in the world that remake the unwilling. That harvest people. That introduce cold into blood and call it sacred and build theologies around the right of the powerful to consume the powerless. These things are not to be tolerated, negotiated with, or forgiven into inaction. They are to be found. They are to be purified. With fire, with force, with the patient and absolute certainty of someone who has already survived the worst thing and is no longer afraid of the cost. 


#### Taboos 

Never inflict on the powerless what was inflicted on you — the fire purifies, it does not consume indiscriminately. A cleric of Mortheon who turns the force of the faith against those it was given to protect has committed the defining sin of the faith. The fire does not burn the field to kill one weed.

  
Never let the cold win quietly — surrender to the cold that violation introduces — the apathy, the cruelty, the slow decision that feeling is no longer worth the effort — is the only form of apostasy Mortheon recognizes. It is choosing to become what was done to you. The one betrayal he will not forgive because it is the one thing he was made specifically to prevent. 


Never destroy what can be redeemed — purification is not obliteration. The goal is not ash for its own sake. It is the removal of corruption so that what lives beneath it can survive. A cleric who mistakes destruction for purification has confused the fire for the purpose of the fire.
