# **AETHARION**

**![[06-Media/Pantheon/Culture 1.png|393]]**

**Epithets 

• The Tablecaller — most common among common folk 

• Mother of Peace — used by scholars and theologians 

• Guardian of the Open Door — used by travelers and the displaced 

• The Storyteller- used by bards, sages, and keepers of tradition  

**The name “Aetharion” is said to predate written language — it appears carved into the oldest standing doorframes, communal wells, and feasting stones across every culture in the world, as if different peoples arrived at the same name independently.**


# DETAILS

Worshipped by: Diplomats, healers, bards, architects, community leaders, refugees, families, peacemakers.

# SYMBOL


> [!NOTE] **The Isos**
>
An open hand, palm outward, set within a ring of seven evenly spaced dots. The hand is centered, fingers together, a gesture of welcome rather than supplication. The seven dots circle the outer edge at perfectly equal intervals — no dot larger or more prominent than another, no position readable as the head.
> [**![[06-Media/Pantheon/Culture 2.png|351]]**

**Simple folk wear a small carved wooden disk with a ring of notches around the edge representing equal seats at the table. 

Clergy wear cast bronze medallions showing the full Isos. 

High priests carry a ritual cloth — always embroidered with the Isos — that they spread over any surface to consecrate it as sacred ground.

# APPEARANCE

Aetharion manifests as a figure of indeterminate age and gender — simultaneously ancient and vital, neither man nor woman but something that contains both and transcends the distinction entirely. They appear enduring, like a great oak at full maturity. 

Physical Form 

• Deeply tanned skin with a faint warm glow beneath, as if sunlight is stored just under the surface. 

• Hair like dark wood shot through with threads of copper and amber, worn loose or loosely braided with dried flowers and carved beads from a dozen cultures. 

• Eyes that shift between deep forest green and warm amber — green when at rest or joyful, amber when moved by sorrow or resolve. 

• A face observers almost never describe the same way twice — some see a weathered elder, some a person in their prime, some see features that resemble their own culture’s idea of a wise ancestor.

• Broad, unhurried hands — a host’s hands, not a warrior’s — always described as warm to the touch. 

• Always bare-footed in divine appearances, said to feel the earth of every community they walk through. 

Aetharion wears layered robes that read as neither masculine nor feminine — deep earth tones, bark brown and umber — cinched with a rope belt from which hang small tokens: a carved bird, a ring of keys, dried herbs, a child’s drawing pressed flat. These tokens shift and change; worshippers who receive visions sometimes recognize their own lost keepsakes among them. 

When Aetharion appears, the air smells of fresh bread, warm stone, and green things after rain. Animals calm. Strangers feel the inexplicable urge to introduce themselves and pull up a chair.

# COMMANDMENTS/TENETS


**The Five Pillars of the Isos**

 I. Set a Place for the Stranger The table is not full until everyone has a seat. Seek out the isolated, the displaced, and the grieving. Bring them to the circle. A community that turns inward will starve itself of everything that makes life worth living.

II. Culture is Memory Made Living Preserve what communities build — their songs, their recipes, their stories, their funerary rites. To let a culture die forgotten is a wound upon the world. Learn from every people you encounter. 

III. Peace is Not Silence True peace is not the absence of conflict but the presence of understanding. You are not required to accept injustice quietly. Speak hard truths. Demand dignity. But do so as a builder, not a destroyer. 

IV. The Door Must Remain Open No follower of Aetharion may turn away someone who comes seeking shelter, healing, or community in earnest. The open door is sacred. Close it only against those who would overturn the table itself. 

V. Violence is the Last Language You may defend the community, the table, and the helpless. But you may not strike first. You may not make war for glory or gain. Every act of violence must be answered for in prayer and service, even when justified. 

  

**Taboos** 

• Never destroy a place of cultural memory — libraries, shrines, community halls, graveyards, or sacred sites, even those of enemy peoples. Defiling the dead’s memory is among the gravest sins. 

• Never break a sworn peace — an oath of ceasefire or treaty made in Aetharion’s name is inviolable. Violation severs divine connection immediately. 

• Never refuse shelter to the genuinely desperate — turning away the wounded, the refugee, or the grief-stricken is a desecration of the open table.

• Never use healing as leverage — a healer of Aetharion does not withhold aid to coerce, punish, or profit. 

• Never claim the head of the table — no follower may declare themselves supreme authority over a community’s culture, identity, or right to exist. Hierarchy within the church is administrative, never spiritual supremacy
