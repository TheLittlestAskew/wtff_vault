# **AURETHRA
 (The Unfinished Hand)**

**![[06-Media/Pantheon/Craft 1.png|413]]**


**Aurethra is the goddess of craft in all its forms: weaving, smithing, carving, painting, drawing, stitching/embroidery, pottery, glasswork, calligraphy, masonry, jewelry-making, woodworking, and any art shaped by devoted hands**


---

# SYMBOL

Her holy symbol is an unfinished circle made from craft tools (curved sewing needle and thread, a smith’s hammer, a chisel, brush, awl, spindle, and carving knife. In temples, the exact tools vary depending on the local artisans who worship her.) The circle is almost complete, but there is always a small gap at the top, symbolizing that the journey of artistry is never truly finished. At the center of the unfinished circle is a pair of open hands glowing with golden-white light, palms facing upward as though offering both a blessing and a challenge.

 Followers of Aurethra often wear this symbol as a pendant, pin, embroidered patch, or carved token.

# APPEARANCE

Aurethra appears as a serene woman with warm, luminous skin, dark (black) braided hair threaded with beads, pins, charms, scraps of ribbon, tiny bells, and fragments of gold wire. Her clothing looks handmade and ever-changing: layered robes, aprons, veils, shawls, leather cuffs, embroidered hems, and tool belts, all beautifully worn from use. Her most striking feature is always her hands. They glow with soft light, as if lit from within by forge-fire, moonlight, candle flame, and morning sun all at once. Her fingertips are stained with pigment (paints), ink, clay, soot, dye, wax, and metal dust. No matter what craft she appears connected to, her hands are never pristine. They are the hands of someone who creates. To different craft-makers, one detail of her appearance changes:

- A smith sees sparks drifting from her hair.
    
- A seamstress sees golden thread wrapped around her wrists.
    
- A painter sees faint brushstrokes shifting across her robes.
    
- A potter sees wet clay beneath the fingernails of her glowing hands.
    
- A woodcarver smells cedar and sees carved bangles on her arms.
    
- A jeweler sees a small, delicate gold circlet-crown worked with fine filigree and set with tiny gemstones on her head.

# COMMANDMENTS/TENANTS

- Create with Care: Every object crafted by hand carries a piece of its maker’s spirit. Ex: A humble spoon carved with love is holier than a jeweled crown made with cruelty.

- Honor the Process: Followers must respect practice, failure, revision, and growth. A flawed beginning is sacred if it is honestly made. Destroying unfinished work out of shame is discouraged unless the materials are being respectfully reclaimed.

- Teach What You Know: Aurethra requires her followers to pass on their skills. Hoarding knowledge of a craft for pride or profit is considered spiritually dangerous. A true master leaves behind students, not just masterpieces.

- Repair Before Replacing: Mending is considered one of the holiest acts. Her followers are expected to repair broken tools, torn garments, damaged homes, wounded relationships, and neglected communities whenever they can.

- Let No Tool Be Abused: Tools are extensions of the crafter’s hand. Followers must keep their tools clean, respected, and properly used. Using a tool to intentionally ruin honest work is a grave offense.

  

#### Taboos (Her followers must not):

- Claim another crafter/artisan’s work as their own.

- Destroy handmade objects out of spite, jealousy, or contempt.

- Mock beginners for imperfect work.

- Create shoddy goods meant to deceive or endanger others.

- Refuse to teach a worthy student out of vanity.

- Use a craft to enslave, exploit, or humiliate.

- Break a tool in anger.

- Leave a creation unsigned.

- The gravest taboo is soulless imitation: copying another’s work without reverence, transformation, permission, or understanding. To Aurethra, imitation used for learning is natural. Theft disguised as craft is sacrilege.
