# Hearthread Hall

## Description
A sprawling, generations-old tavern in **[[Rhusatatiam]]**, capital of the **[[Rhus Valley]]** — a social anchor of the valley. Timber beams wrapped in living ivy, river stone, no two chairs matching, with quilts and standards from valley towns hung from the rafters. It is built in **three tiers**:
- **First floor (main tavern floor)** — holds the **Common Fire** and most of the drinkers; site of the joint tavern social in S02.
- **Second floor** — open-plan with a small stage; site of [[The Uprooters]] interviews (S01) and live music and dancing (S02).
- **Private upper-floor room** — [[Aiphrumfite ‘Aiph’|Aiph]]’s personal space: warm and lived-in, with a blanket kicked off a couch, a wine goblet still out, a book left face-down. Used for Group K orientation in S02.
- **Third floor** — exists above; where Aiph and [[Margaret “Mags” HoneyThatcher|Mags]] held their private negotiation (S01).
- **Exterior (back)** — outside the tavern at night; cold air with a chill; [[Tobias Wolfe|Tobias]] painted his personal mark on the exterior wall here in S02.

## Notable Features
- **The Common Fire** — a central hearth "never allowed to die," first lit with ash, wood, and flame contributed by every major [[Rhus Valley]] town. Travelers leave offerings: an herb sprig, a written memory, a coin, a song.
- **The bar** *(S07)* — about 20–25 ft from the Common Fire tables (Adam). Served by a tall **dwarven waiter** (unnamed; Adam-voiced) and a **bartender** (unnamed; Alec-voiced; pronoun inconsistent in the transcript). In S07 Alec describes the tavern as loud, crowded and familiar, with salted meats, ale, card games and dancing.
- ⚠️ *(S07)* Alec calls the venue the **"Hearthread Tavern"**; this page's canon name is Hearthread Hall (F59). Confirm with Adam.

## Inhabitants
A mixed crowd of travelers, farmers, musicians, and scribes. In [[Session 01 — Winds in the East, Mist comin' in...]] it hosted the candidates and recruiters of [[The Uprooters]].

## History
[Unknown/Ambiguous] — generations old; its Common Fire predates living memory as a communal symbol of the valley.
