> [!quote]- Session 07 — Omission of Truth (09/20/2026)
> *[[Session 07 — Omission of Truth|Back at the fire, and the city that smells wrong]]*
>
> We came back to where it started. The same tavern, the same fire in the middle of it that nobody lets die. It isn't morning and it isn't evening. Afternoon, warm, loud.
>
> The machine is moving again. It was frozen solid the last time I really looked, and now it's fussing over the snail that has been riding its arm since before the dungeon, like nothing happened. The quiet girl stood up from the fire and ran upstairs without a word to anyone. The machine watched her go and said it wasn't a normal kind of running. I believe it. I don't know what it was.
>
> Then Aiph sat down with us and asked a favor. Don't tell them. She and the dragon lady built those tests together, and she doesn't want the dragon lady knowing it was hers that went wrong. Hey, we did it, she said, we crushed it, we're Uprooters now. And then she was gone again.
>
> So that's what we are. Uprooters. I thought it would feel like more.
>
> The others chewed on the asking for a long time. The machine doesn't understand hiding a truth and said so: leaving something out is still not the truth. The tall one wanted to know *why*. The cold one said he'd tell whatever lie Aiph needed, because she's the only one who hasn't looked at him like he's broken. I don't think it's a contest between the two recruiters, not the way the others were turning it over. Something got into her dungeon that she didn't put there. What she meant for us got killed by something she never meant for us at all. That's a hole in her fence. Nobody wants their hole in the fence talked about. So: don't say anything. That's easy. I'm good at not saying anything.
>
> A dwarf came for drinks. I asked for wine and got red. The cold one asked for fire whiskey and the dwarf told him, kindly enough, that he didn't look like he could handle it. The machine asked for used cooking grease. When the dwarf came back he had a whole bottle of red and a whole bottle of the whiskey and a little wooden bowl of grease, and he said he was sorry, that he hadn't known who we were. Then he ran. People are going to start knowing who we are. I don't know how I feel about that yet.
>
> The machine had been knitting something this whole time, and it turned out to be a bag that holds more than a bag should. The wizard and the tall one both looked at it like it had grown teeth. The wizard put our heavy gold in it. I'm glad not to be the one carrying it.
>
> The other group came in behind the dragon lady. She told them to behave and have a drink on her, and then she went straight to Aiph at the bar. I kept to my seat. I said I'd keep my distance from that one and I meant it.
>
> One of the other group, a woman who's sweet on the machine, sat down at our table and started asking it questions. I thought she was asking me. I stood up halfway to answer, and the cold one had to tell me she was talking to the machine about its snail. Fine. As far as I'm concerned we're lying, and everybody at that table was acting strange about it, so I'm done. I looked over at Aiph on my way up. She saw me stand. She looked away fast, like she hadn't. I only wanted her to see me go. I hope she remembers what we said to each other on the train.
>
> I took the wine. All of it. Nobody needs to drink at me.
>
> Outside, I found a place with something growing in it, like the last time I was here, and sat with my back to it. And it was wrong. I don't know how else to put it. This whole city is grown over: flowers down the walls, vines over the brick, leaves pouring off the corners of things. I know what a forest feels like. I know what a garden feels like. This isn't either one. It doesn't feel right, and I sat in it and couldn't make it feel right, and I didn't like how long I sat there trying.
>
> Two of the other group came out of the tavern while I was sitting with that. Later, the wizard came out too, bolting through the doors like something was on fire. I don't know what he was running at. I didn't go after him.
>
> I'm still out here. The bottle's lighter than it was. Whatever is happening at that bar, it's between the two of them, and I'll hear about it whether I want to or not.
>
> *Leave it out, and it's still not the truth. The machine is right about that. I'll keep my mouth shut anyway.*

> [!quote]- Session 05 — The Bloody Truth (08/09/2026)
> *[[Session 05 — The Bloody Truth|It got back up, and then it didn’t]]*
>
> Two of them came out of the head. Smaller than the first, both going straight for the cold one, and he just wasn't there when they landed. Dodge, dodge, then his hand across one's face and a line of ice on its cheek, and he stepped back behind the tall one like he was dancing. I've watched him flinch at a ladder. I don't know who that was.
>
> I told them we'd need fire. He said fire's not his thing. Not mine either. I went in anyway: the axe across the chest of the one on the right, and when that wasn't enough I bit it. Teeth are teeth. The machine opened up its wrists and sprayed something slick around them both, and there were still embers from the wizard's little bomb, and up they went. Screaming. Burning. Good.
>
> Then the dead thing moved. The big body. Its head lifted and dropped and the ground shook, and the tall one and the wizard went down. I thought it was dead. I still think it was dead. Bodies do that sometimes.
>
> Second time, I didn't wait to see what it would grow. Both hands over my head, straight down, and I left the ground to do it. The head came off and rolled. I landed ready, because I wasn't going to be surprised twice in one day. The other one leapt at me and slid sideways in a blue outline and missed, and I know whose hand that was. Then the quiet girl stood back with her sling and put a stone through its head.
>
> I put my ear to the dragon. No heartbeat. Nothing.
>
> Then everyone started taking it apart. The machine took hide and blood and an eye. The tall one ripped out a rib to make a weapon. The cold one took teeth, flesh, a piece of the tail. The quiet girl looked at us like we were monsters, and the wizard called it somebody's father. It isn't. This is nature. Something ate it, the way things eat things. You don't waste what's left. I told the tall one everyone should get two ribs, and when she lifted the whole chest of gold one-handed like it weighed nothing, I carried her bones for her instead.
>
> The wizard ate one of the little colored balls from the jar. Of course he did. And then he put his hands out and I felt the scrape on me close up. So that one's useful. We're keeping the rest.
>
> I knelt by the body to see what killed it. Something ate it. That's all I got. The machine turned its whole head and looked at me and didn't say anything, which said plenty.
>
> The cold one stayed behind when the rest of us moved to the door. I saw him. He reached into the dragon's chest and took the heart, and put it in his bag, and made sure the quiet girl wasn't looking first. I didn't say anything. It's his.
>
> Up top, she was sitting under her tent with a notebook, staring at the trapdoor. She looked at us and said, *No way.* Then she clapped and grabbed the tall one's hand and said we were so much faster than the other group. She said she chose the best group. Then the cold one showed her his drawings, and she stopped being happy. She has never seen either of those things before. Not the dragon. Not the thing eating it. She paced. She said most groups don't make it all the way through, and she's never walked every room herself.
>
> So I asked her the obvious thing. Whatever was in there, was that what's been killing the ones who went before us? She said it was a good question. She didn't answer it.
>
> At the inn she drank my shot. Just picked it up and threw it back, then the quiet girl's, and said she'd see us in the morning, and walked off. Fine. I got my own drink.
>
> The machine held the dragon's scale up to me, and I nearly fell out of my chair. The bug's eye smelled like nothing. But the scale. It's like the difference between a room someone sprayed orange into and having the orange shoved up your nose. That close. That loud. I sat there with my drink trying to make it mean something. I don't know yet if it does.
>
> The cold one made the server a rose out of ice. She said nothing lasts. Then the machine tapped its chest stone and said *dungeon clear*, and a little later it went still and upset. Its friend in the other group is in danger. Running for their life. Nobody knew what to say to that.
>
> Then the cold one went blank over the blue stone. When he came back he said he'd been at the Crater, and then in a room, watching an elf write like his life depended on it. He asked why. The elf said, *because it does.* The wizard gave him the green stone to try, and it took them both, and then the quiet girl too. I watched the cold one and the quiet girl reach across the table and hold hands without knowing they were doing it. When they came back, he said it was a man with green eyes and a rough voice, arguing with someone he loved, ready to burn everything for them. He says it was neither of our two recruiters. The wizard just wrote and wrote. The cold one drew it like the drawing was the only thing keeping him in his chair. The machine looked at the picture a long time and said there is hidden knowledge in it.
>
> He called me intense and capable, earlier. I'll take it.
>
> And then the machine pushed. It said it only wants the truth, and the cold one gave it the truth. Left on a fountain in the worst winter anyone remembers. An orphanage. A headmistress who pulled his sketchbooks out of the water when the other kids threw them in. A broken arm, and a town that froze behind him while a friend carried him for help, and after that they called him a demon. Two maybe-friends who wouldn't come with him. He said he doesn't know anything about love.
>
> The table went to ice while he talked. The drinks. Our feet. Everyone in the whole room, stuck where they stood. I couldn't move. He looked up and saw what he'd done, and said *I'm sorry, I'm sorry,* and ran.
>
> Everything let go when he was through the door. And then he was just gone.
>
> *Nobody could move when he ran. Next time I'll be faster.*

> [!quote]- Session 04 — Four Blooms, Five Petals (07/26/2026)
> *[[Session 04 — Four Blooms, Five Petals|Five rooms down, and the thing that was eating]]*
>
> The ladder was gone the second we let go of it. Just cave wall above, nothing to climb back up. I can see in the dark and most of them can't, and even so, the tall one and the quiet girl and the wizard spotted the pillars before I did. Stone columns around the edges, with writing on them.
>
> The writing was Druidic. I can read it. So can the quiet girl. That's two of us, out of six.
>
> Mine said: *We guards can be short, we guards can be tall, and without guards like me the whole city would fall.* Walls. Easy. So I put my hand on it and said it, in the old tongue, and the room answered by throwing something at us. Fast things, from every pillar at once. I felt them go into me, little and sharp, and when I looked down there was nothing there. No hole, no blood. It still hurt.
>
> The cold one figured it out. It hadn't hit us right away; it waited, like it wanted the other answers. So we'd have to say them all together. I don't like riddles. I like knowing where the teeth are. But the tall one had the last one (silence), the wizard had cloud, and the arrows were the arrows. The quiet girl and I taught them how to say the words right. I let the anger come up before I touched my pillar again, just in case. Walls. Arrows. Cloud. Silence.
>
> And then there was no sound at all.
>
> Everyone's mouths moving, nothing coming out. I hate that. I hate it worse than the dark. Then the machine turned to me and started talking with its hands, and I could have laughed. Nobody ever needs that language, and there it was, the only one that worked down there. The machine had worked out that each trap was the answer to the next riddle. Somebody should have told me that's how they build these. I told it everybody should learn to talk with their hands. It agreed.
>
> A hole had opened in the middle of the floor. The cold one sent one of his little bird-lights down it and the light just went out. The wizard sent his bird down and couldn't feel it anymore. The machine stuck its arm in and pulled it back out whole, and then it crawled in. That's the thing about the machine. It looks at a hole that eats light and decides it's lying.
>
> It was lying. Ten feet of drop and soft dirt at the bottom. A room full of grass and trees. The wizard asked out loud why the machine had jumped down a hole and a vine came up out of the ground and smacked him for it, then again. So: quiet. The cold one tied a cloth over his own mouth.
>
> Every tree had a little light hidden behind it, a pulsing ball. Touch the ball and the tree walks. The machine told me with its hands that we had to walk the trees to the middle. I told it we should just pick the trees up. It said no, the balls. Fine. I went quiet to mine and grabbed it, and the tree went barreling for the center like it had been waiting its whole life to get there. The others did theirs. The trees folded into each other like they were stretching, and the soft ground in the middle dropped away into black, and their branches knit into a ramp going down. When I set my light back at the roots, the branches curled around it, and something warm went back into me. A thank-you, maybe. The machine put both its hands on the tree and thanked it first.
>
> Next room: six little beds of flowers around a cauldron, and the words *four blooms, five petals* on the side. The wizard pulled the answer out of a grid of letters on a scrap of paper in the corner. Gold, black, purple, gold, white. One petal each into the pot, and every one smelled better than the last, until it smelled like the best thing I know. Then cups. The machine poured it over itself, went through every color it has, settled on dark purple, and vanished. The tall one drank and vanished. I drank. It felt like being warm and fed and done for the day. Then I was walking in the dark toward a light.
>
> A big door at the top of some steps, covered in sculpted hands. The cold one said his headmistress used to say the devil doesn't come with horns, he comes to the front door in a suit and a smile. Then the quiet girl pulled the hands off one by one until they all came down and rotted to dust, and there was just a big wooden door. Five of us leaned on it. It didn't give until the sixth put a hand on it.
>
> Inside: a dome, moss of every color, a chest in the middle. I didn't see what was behind the chest. The cold one did. He grabbed the tall one by the back of her armor before she walked right up to it: a huge lizard skeleton, half flesh, bright purple and pink, flower petals all over it. The quiet girl heard chewing. The wizard's bird went and looked. Something red and many-legged, part centipede, part snail, part fly, bigger than any of us, eating that body so hard it was swollen with it.
>
> Finally.
>
> The cold one wanted to draw it. I told him if he picked up a pencil I'd hit him. He said a picture could help fuel the legend. Fine. You do that, and we'll fight the thing, and if it comes for you, you're on your own.
>
> I walked out in front of everybody. Slowly. Not a threat, just walking. Halfway there it lifted its head and tilted it at me, and I knew. It came over the body in one leap.
>
> The cold one hit it first: a blast of ice that froze one of its legs solid. Better than a drawing, I told him. Then I let the bear up. They'd never seen it. I felt myself get bigger and heavier, heard the sound come out of my chest, and I closed the distance and swung, and missed. So I stood there. I'm not coming to you. You come to me.
>
> It came. It dug a claw into me and pulled me in close and held on. The quiet girl threw ice at it and the thing twisted and splashed the cold straight into me, and she panicked, and then her voice put some of me back together. Twice, before the end. I told her she's a goddess. I meant it.
>
> I got a hand on the claw and tore it out of myself, and kept hold of it, and brought the axe down and took a leg off. It screamed and ran for cover behind the body, and I cut it again as it went. The wizard threw something small over the corpse and clapped, and it lit up and rolled, and then it went off. Red meat came up over the top. The tall one finally got close enough, looked at it like you'd look at a dying thing, said she guessed she had to put it out of its misery, and smashed its head flat.
>
> I celebrated. I'm not going to pretend I didn't.
>
> The machine looked at the big body and said the meat was fresh. Whatever it was, it died recently, or nearly had, and this thing had been eating it. The tall one said it looked like a dragon.
>
> Then the cold one went to pick teeth out of the mess, and the smashed head started to bubble. A new head came out of it. Then another one. Both of them went for him.
>
> *Riddles all day. Then one good fight, and it got back up. Of course it did.*

> [!quote]- Session 03 — Raise a Glass to Provisional Trust (07/12/2026)
> *[[Session 03 — Raise a Glass to Provisional Trust|On the green train, and the mouth in the ground]]*
>
> **Day one — the train.**
>
> They gave us a train the color of a hothouse. Aiph walked us through it like it was a house she'd lived in a long time: a car for eating, a car for growing things, a car full of soft chairs and books, cabins, a room at the back that is all one window. She handed each of us a little wooden coin with the mark on it, said it would pay our way, said the ride was a few hours. Her pocket watch is broken. She said that like it was funny.
>
> The others scattered like water finding cracks. The cold one and his fox went looking for sweets. The quiet girl went to the growing-car to be near green things, and the machine and the tall one followed. The wizard went off toward the back, curious about the workings of the train, the way he's curious about everything.
>
> I stayed. I took out my axe and worked the edge until it was right. It settles me, the same way pacing a den settles an animal. And Aiph was in that car too, at the far end, writing in a little book.
>
> I have wanted to ask her something since the tavern, so I moved close, quiet, and I asked. Her partner — the dragon lady — I could smell something on her the whole night and I didn't have a word for it. Aiph put her book down. She said I smell the *shifting of the very world*. She said the dragon lady is shifting too, and she hoped it frightened her, because that one hates change.
>
> And it landed in me all at once, the way a thing does when someone finally names it. I have smelled that smell my whole life. I never knew what it was. It's the smell of something *becoming* something else. It's why the water stopped running the way it used to. It's why I left. It's why I'm here. Naming it was a relief, like the ache after you finally set a bone.
>
> She told me more than I asked for. She said there is going to be violence before this is done, and that's part of why she chose me. I told her I can do violence — I'll do it to learn what's happening — but I needed to know her partner's fear wouldn't come down on my head. She wouldn't lie to me. She said the dragon lady is stronger than she is, and that all she could promise was to *try*, to get a chance to talk first. That's not nothing. It's not a wall between me and a thing with teeth, either. So I decided: I'll keep my distance. As long as she doesn't close it on purpose, we'll be fine. I don't want to fight someone who's built like me.
>
> Then I went and got food that wasn't a carrot. Meat on the bone, and something small and strong to drink. It burns going down the same as it burns in a wound — I told the old man behind the bar that, and he knew exactly what I meant. I ate the way I eat. Fast, clean, nothing wasted. The quiet girl looked like she might be sick watching me. That's fine.
>
> The train slowed. A town. A sign that said this was where endings took root and new things grew. Pretty words for a place with a hole in the ground.
>
> **Day one — the inn.**
>
> Aiph walked us over a bridge into a loud, warm place with a band in the middle and a bar on every wall. She'll sleep here too, she said, doing her reading. Tomorrow, the test.
>
> At the table the cold one started worrying at trust like a dog with a bone. He doesn't trust the recruiters. He doesn't trust *us* — says we don't know each other, so how can we do this. The wizard's bird overheard the two women arguing back at the tavern; one of them said she didn't think any of us were good enough. That's what's eating him.
>
> I don't understand the worry. Nobody starts a journey knowing what they're doing. You assume trust and you take it back only when someone earns you taking it back. I told them the truth, too, so it wouldn't sit wrong: I would sell every one of them for the people who matter to my life. That doesn't mean we can't work together. It just means I know where I stand, and now they do.
>
> The machine had the cleanest word for it: *provisional trust.* Trust you give because the moment calls for it, and keep until you're given a reason not to. It put its arm in the middle of the table. One by one the others laid hands in — the tall one, the quiet girl, the wizard, and last, and slowest, the cold one, who has clearly had trust cost him before. I didn't put my hand in. Not because I don't trust them. I do. I just don't do that. I told them so. Some of them were watching the room while it happened, and two of them caught Aiph at the bar, raising her glass at us like she'd seen something she liked.
>
> The cold one gave the tall one a drawing of her hammer. She folded it away against her body like it mattered. It did.
>
> I went up to my room when a body should — I'm made for the morning and I know it.
>
> **Day two — the mouth in the ground.**
>
> Aiph led us up through the early market to a ruin that didn't want to be noticed, and in the middle of it, a trap door. Five layers down, a chest at the bottom. Bring up what's inside. She'll wait at the top under a little tent, and she told us — plainly, like she was surprised to hear herself say it — that she really is rooting for us, that a day or two ago she didn't think we were ready, and part of her still doesn't, but she's more hopeful than she was.
>
> The tall one opened it. Black all the way down. I didn't take the ladder. I jumped. The others came after, and last of all the machine climbed down on all its legs like a careful spider. Then the door above us shut, and the light went with it, and we stood in an empty stone room in the dark, waiting to find out what she built for us.
>
> *Provisional trust. It'll do.*

> [!quote]- Session 02 — Something's Changing (06/28/2026)
> *[[Session 02 — It Follows Me, So I Followed It|The night we got on a train]]*
>
> They put us in a room. Not the big noisy one downstairs — a smaller one upstairs, warm and a little messy in the way that means someone actually lives there. A blanket kicked off the couch. A wine glass still sitting out. The kind of mess that says a real person sleeps here, not a performance of a real person.
>
> Aiph gathered us in. She had drinks brought up, and she asked us, one by one, why we were here and not somewhere else.
>
> The machine speaks in third person. Said its name is something complicated — botanical, a whole phrase — and it loves water even though it cannot drink it. Said its heart fails it. There's something at the center of its chest that pulses like a slow ember. When it held up its hands to show me what I was trying to figure out when I picked it up, I noticed: the light from its center is the same color as its eyes. I don't know if that's a coincidence or the truest thing I've ever seen.
>
> The wizard has a bird that cannot die. He killed it to prove a point — sent it straight into a closed window — and it faded like smoke and came back whole. He held my — he held the cold man's hand, and his face did something I recognized: the brace against a thing that surprises you. Afterward he started scribbling in a little book.
>
> The cold man. Tobias. He paints. He goes frost when he's frightened. His fox is the same way — alive past the point where alive should end. He seems not to know yet that this makes him remarkable. He thinks it makes him a problem.
>
> The one who asks questions about old things. Zarna. She said she's a museum person, really, but said *archaeologist* first. She said it like she was trying it on.
>
> And Eliza. Eliza was so quiet I almost didn't see her, and then something crossed her face when Aiph asked why she came, and I thought: that look. I know that look. Someone who came looking and is not ready to say looking for what.
>
> When my turn came, I hesitated. Last time I answered that question I said something people didn't like. But Aiph caught it — she said *you've already passed the test*, and somehow that was the thing I needed to hear. So I told her the only truth I know how to give: something is changing. Changing. I don't know what it is, but it follows me, and I followed it here.
>
> She nodded. She seemed like she already knew.
>
> Aiph told us about the hole at the center of this world. She said groups have gone before us — some didn't come back, some came back and didn't last much past coming back. She said we were chosen. She said we were extraordinary. I didn't argue with her, but I kept my head down.
>
> Tobias asked about the Age of Heroes coming back. Aiph's face went flat in a way that wasn't cold — it was scared. *Heroes are good things in stories,* she said. *In actuality, all they bring is destruction.*
>
> When she left the room she stopped at the door. Turned. Looked at each of us. Said, *I need you to trust me. It can't come back.* Then she walked out.
>
> We talked after. The machine — Bebo — let me hold it. It went completely still when I picked it up, like it was trusting me with the whole weight of itself, and it is very heavy. I set it down exactly where I found it. Bebo turned to face me and extended a limb. I reached out my hand. We shook. Its eyes went to the shape eyes make when something is glad.
>
> Later there were more people. The other group came down to the tavern and everything got louder and more interesting and harder to track. Someone handed the cold man a piece of paper and he read it and a hand made of ice appeared in the air and then shattered. The dragon lady sat down next to him and asked if she scared him and he said yes and she got up and left. I think that was the point.
>
> Bebo ended up with snails on it. I ate one. It tasted like nothing and everything at the same time.
>
> The other group's person — the one with the compass — cried outside in the dark. I didn't know that then. I only found out later from the way people moved around a door upstairs.
>
> I sat outside for a while. Just breathing. The air was cold and clear and smelled of the wrong season, which is still something. I can work with something.
>
> Morning. A platform. Two trains. We all stood there knowing this was the last time all twelve of us would be in the same place for a long while. The cold man and the compass girl made an agreement about marks — he paints a symbol on places he's been so his friends at home can trace him if they ever leave; she wanted to be part of the trail. I watched them press ink into each other's arms with their fingers.
>
> The machine and the girl from the other group — Nyx — said goodbye to each other. Bebo tapped the place in its chest where the stone had sunk in. *Never be far.*
>
> I don't know what Sunroute Crossing holds. I know that I'm going there with a machine that trusts me with its full weight, a wizard who kills his bird for science, a painter made of cold, a woman pretending she's only here by accident, and a quiet one who is looking for something. And me. Smelling for something I can't name.
>
> Something is changing. I followed it here, and now I'm getting on a train.

> I have never been so far from the water and felt it so loudly.

Nobody hears my feet when I don't want them to, and tonight I wanted to be a draft under a door, nothing more.
>
> But the air was wrong. It has been wrong for a long while now, this thing I came so far to chase — the smell that rides on water that no longer runs the way it should. I thought I'd left it behind me on the road. I hadn't. It was here too, in a tavern packed with people who couldn't smell it at all.
>
> Two of them were running this gauntlet up on the stage. One drew a circle in chalk and the noise of the whole world fell away inside it — cold, careful, the kind of person who measures you before you've finished sitting down. The other was a storm in a body: a bag spilling odd little things, a pinch of sand that caught fire in her fingers and changed color and changed again, a laugh sharp enough to cut. They called the rest up one by one. A scholar with golden eyes. A girl who hands out flowers. A painted boy with a fox on his shoulder. A turtle-child folding paper animals that breathed for a moment and then went still. A great gentle giant who broke a chair and then mended it, apologizing the whole time.
>
> When my name was called I looked for a sign before I sat. She gave me one. And then she asked how far home was, and something in me decided to be honest. I told her the water doesn't run like it used to. That there's a scent on the air, and most people don't know it's there, and I need to know what it is and why it's reaching my home.
>
> She went still. I smell like that scent,* she said. So I took the bear down off my head and let her see the rest of my face, and I told her the truth I'd been carrying since the road: *I know. I can smell it from here.*
>
> For just a breath the whole table changed. Whatever they'd planned, I'd stepped off the edge of it. The cold one and the storm had a quarrel after I left — quiet, but I felt the heat of it on my neck. Before I went she told me the only thing that has stayed with me all night: *trust your nose.* Then she told me to go away, so I did. I perched by the door. When the room got loud and warm and people started pressing in to learn each other's names, I slipped outside, where I could breathe and listen.
>
> I don't know what I am to these people yet. When the choosing came, I felt the night tip over into something, and I missed it — too busy walking back through the door to read the look that passed between the two of them. But my name was on the storm's list. Whatever this is, she wants me in it. She wanted me *because* of the scent, I think.
>
> I came to find out what is wrong with the world. I think I just agreed to walk straight into it.
